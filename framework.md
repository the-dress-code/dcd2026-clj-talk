# Compass:

Clojure was my first language, and it didn't just teach me to code — it taught me how to think.

## Days 1-3:

 Nail your story arc. Just the skeleton — what happens in each act, in plain sentences. No slides.

## Days 4-8:

Talk it out loud. Not reading slides — telling the story, to a wall, a friend, a voice memo. Find out where you lose steam, where you light up, where you go blank. That tells you what's real and what's filler.

## Days 9-14:
Now build slides around what survived.

## Days 15-20:
Rehearse for real. Time it. Cut what puts you over.

-----------------------------------

## Day 1

30-45 minutes max:

Write down your three acts as three short paragraphs. Not bullet points, not slide headings — write it the way you'd tell a friend over coffee. Something like:

Act I: "So here's the thing — I didn't come from tech. I came from creative arts. I started with a puzzle game, then pivoted to Clojure on a recommendation. I found community that kept me accountable. I struggled, but the struggles weren't really Clojure-specific — they were programming struggles."

Act II: Pick your top two features. Write a paragraph for each: what it is, and a specific moment when it helped you. Not a definition — a moment.

Act III: "Here's what stuck..." — one or two concrete ways your thinking changed. Even if they're rough.

That's it. Go to bed.

## Day 2

### Tomorrow before the call:
Read your paragraphs out loud once or twice. Then put them down and just talk through it on the call. Tell your friend the story. Let yourself stumble. The point of this dry run isn't to perform — it's to find out where the story flows naturally and where you get stuck. That information is worth more than any slide you could make tonight.

### After the call,
come back and tell me what worked and where you stalled. That's when we start building for real.
