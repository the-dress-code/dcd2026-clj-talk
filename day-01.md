## Day 1

30-45 minutes max:

Write down your three acts as three short paragraphs. Not bullet points, not slide headings — write it the way you'd tell a friend over coffee. Something like:

Act I: So here's the thing — I didn't come from tech. I came from creative arts. I started with a puzzle game, then pivoted to Clojure on a recommendation. I found community that kept me accountable. I struggled, but the struggles weren't really Clojure-specific — they were programming struggles.

Act II: Pick your top two features. Write a paragraph for each: what it is, and a specific moment when it helped you. Not a definition — a moment.

Repl: "Eval to comment for everything — what does this evaluate to?" — that's a REPL habit. When in your life outside of code have you caught yourself doing the equivalent? Breaking something down to check each piece before trusting the whole? Swimsuit.

Claude: That's a good instinct. And notice what you just did — you talked yourself into an answer by reasoning out loud. REPL and minimal syntax. Those are your two.

And the functional stuff (immutability, purity) doesn't disappear — it becomes part of how you talk about the other two. The syntax isn't just minimal, it's minimal because there's no mutation ceremony, no type declarations, no class boilerplate. The REPL isn't just fast feedback, it's fast feedback on pure expressions that you can trust won't change something elsewhere. The functional philosophy is the why behind the features you're keeping. You don't need a dedicated section for it — it's woven in.
